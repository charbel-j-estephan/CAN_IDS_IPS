What you are doing

Five tests on the STM32 that turn the paper's weakest claims into real numbers. Tick every box, type the numbers into the boxes, and press Save results file at the end. Nothing here needs flashing the STM32 or the FPGA.

T1. Held out traffic, prevention on, seed 1

7 min 40,000 frames in blocks of 2,500 from all four attack files, only from the part the model never trained on. The attacker comes back on the bus at every block, so the STM32 has to keep destroying frames all run long. This is the real test of the STM32's timing.

  1. stm32_tests.bat t1

Expected

Almost every attack frame of every class destroyed (Fuzzy may let a few through).
Normal lost: 0 or a few.
In the #STAT line: ilate=0 and dlate=0, and rej far higher than the 21 to 32 of the old runs.
Attacker: pushed off and came back about once per block.

You got

ilate above 0? Some attack verdicts came too late. That is a real result, not a failure: write the number down and keep going. It tells the paper exactly how the STM32 behaves under load.

T2. Held out traffic, seed 2

7 min Same test, different blocks. Two runs give the paper a repeat.

T3. Prevention off

5 min Three short runs with nobody destroying frames, so the paper can show what gets through without a detector. The STM32 stays on the bus but only listens.

  2. stm32_tests.bat t3

Expected

Every attack frame arrives at the receiver.
The attacker never goes bus off.

You got

T4. False flag test

3 min 20,000 genuine frames, no attack. The five byte model wrongly flags 699 of them. Each destroyed frame is resent and destroyed again, which can push the honest sender off the bus. The attacker stays plugged in but sends nothing.

  1. stm32_tests.bat t4

Expected

Many normal frames lost.
Sender: tec high, likely state=busoff. This is the problem strict mode fixes on the FPGA (your partner's T4b).

You got

T5. Long held out run

15 min 200,000 frames. Leave the bench alone while it runs.

Power for the STM32

Nothing to do. The paper uses the datasheet estimate, labeled as an estimate.

ItemValueSource
STM32F407 at 168 MHz, run modeabout 40 mA (peripherals off) to 87 mA (all on)Datasheet, typical, 25 °C
Chip power at 3.3 Vabout 130 to 290 mW, all the time (the CPU never sleeps)Calculated
One model runroughly 6 to 19 µJ (48 µs median, 66 µs max)Calculated

If the university lab has a USB power meter (a small dongle that plugs between the cable and the board), you can add a real reading in 2 minutes: plug it in, run T1, write down the average current and voltage. No cable cutting.

After the tests

If something goes wrong

What you seeWhat to do
"needs attack_sender version 2"Section 1: flash version 2 on the attacker.
"Could not find exactly one sender and one receiver"A Serial Monitor is open, or a USB cable. Close the Arduino IDE, replug the ESP32s.
"did not confirm prevention on"The STM32 is not on the IDS firmware, or its port is busy. Replug it, or run run_demo.bat prepare.
Link check FAILEDBus wiring or terminators. Power off, check about 60 Ω.
Attack frames arrive in T1, T2 or T5Write it down: it is a result. Check the #STAT line for ilate and dlate.
Normal frames lost in T3 (prevention off)Check the FPGA is really off the bus (section 1).
